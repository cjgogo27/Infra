## The engineering problem

BF16 weights for a 7B model appear to need about 14 GB, so you start full training on a 24 GB card. It runs out of memory before completing one step.

> Keep this question in mind: Why does LoRA memory not shrink in proportion to the trainable parameter count?

**Tensor · Activation · Optimizer state · Attention**

> **In one sentence: Weights are only one expense: training also stores gradients, history, and intermediate results.**

A tensor is a multidimensional array with a shape, dtype, and device. [B,S,H] commonly denotes batch, sequence length, and hidden dimension. Equal element counts do not imply equal speed: layout and matrix dimensions affect kernel efficiency.

Transformer Attention combines information from permitted positions; the MLP applies a position-wise nonlinear transformation. Causal masks prevent autoregressive training from reading future answers. Vision encoders may turn image patches into tokens; more images and higher resolution increase computation and activations.

A teaching budget assumes 2-byte BF16 weights, 2-byte gradients, a 4-byte FP32 master copy, and two FP32 Adam moments totaling 8 bytes:
~~~text
Persistent model state ≈ parameter count × (2+2+4+8) bytes
7×10^9 × 16 ≈ 112 GB, using decimal units
~~~
Implementations may omit the master copy or use other gradient precisions or optimizers. Sixteen bytes is an assumption, not a framework law. Activations, workspaces, communication buffers, and allocator reservations are additional. One GiB is 2^30 bytes, unlike decimal GB.

Inference normally omits gradients and optimizer state, but generation may retain a KV cache. LoRA reduces trainable parameters and their optimizer state; base weights and necessary activations remain. Full training, LoRA, and inference need different budgets.

**Upstream and downstream:** Sequence length affects activation and Attention costs. Data-parallel replicas do not automatically reduce the size of each copy.

**LabBot in practice:** When freezing an encoder, decide whether gradient recording can stop. Setting its learning rate to zero does not itself eliminate activations.
