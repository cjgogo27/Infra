## The engineering problem

A small GPU cannot fit a large batch. You reduce the microbatch and accumulate gradients, but update magnitudes become unexpected.

> Keep this question in mind: Four cards, two samples per card, eight accumulation steps: what is the effective batch?

**Backpropagation · Automatic mixed precision (AMP) · Gradient accumulation**

> **In one sentence: Small-batch gradients can form one update only if counting, scaling, and synchronization remain correct.**

An update clears gradients, performs the forward pass, computes loss, backpropagates, and takes an optimizer step. Gradients accumulate in .grad. Failing to clear them carries over previous updates; clearing after every microbatch prevents accumulation.

For equal-length, equally weighted samples, effective batch = per-replica microbatch b × data-parallel replicas D × accumulation steps A. Thus 2×4×8=64. Tensor-parallel cards jointly process a sample and do not multiply the data batch. Equal-weight mean losses are often divided by A. When valid-token counts differ, weight by valid tokens rather than averaging microbatch means equally.

AMP uses lower precision for suitable operations while retaining higher precision for sensitive ones. FP16 has limited range and commonly uses GradScaler. BF16 has a wider exponent range and usually does not need the same scaling, but requires hardware support. Unscale before gradient clipping, then step.

Accumulation need not exactly match one large batch: BatchNorm, randomness, rounding, and weighting can differ. Advance learning-rate schedules according to the chosen optimizer-step or token convention. Normalize a final partial accumulation window correctly.

**Upstream and downstream:** Smaller microbatches reduce activation peaks but may lower GPU efficiency; global batch affects convergence.

**LabBot in practice:** Decide whether variable-length action losses are averaged by action, trajectory, or token. Optimization must preserve that meaning.

Verify it with the [weighted accumulation lab](/Infra/en/labs/05-accumulation/): compare full batches, microbatches with 2/6 valid tokens, and a partial final window on CPU.
