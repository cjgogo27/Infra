## The engineering problem

Offline validation works, but a new camera pipeline breaks the robot. Training used RGB; deployment uses BGR, and action units also differ.

> Keep this question in mind: Why can two action tensors with identical shapes be incompatible?

**Vision-language model (VLM) · Vision-language-action model (VLA) · Action representation**

> **In one sentence: Embodied deployment must preserve time, coordinates, units, and preprocessing—not merely tensor shapes.**

A common VLA encodes images, combines visual and language features through a fusion module or language backbone, and emits action tokens or continuous actions. Architectures vary. Actions can mean joint angles, end-effector pose increments, velocities, or gripper commands, with different ranges and frequencies.

An interface contract should specify image layout, RGB/BGR, normalization, resolution, camera-calibration version, state-field order, action units, coordinate frames, and timestamps. Training normalization statistics cannot arbitrarily be replaced by online-batch statistics.

More cameras and higher resolution increase visual tokens or encoder work. Compression, feature caching, and frame skipping may reduce cost but impair reactions to moving objects. Reusing supposedly static features requires explicit conditions and invalidation.

Distinguish absolute targets from increments. Confusing meters with millimeters or radians with degrees is not necessarily exposed by model loss. Validate offline interfaces, then simulation, then controlled hardware execution.

**Upstream and downstream:** Camera drivers and controllers define input/output meaning; model backends must preserve it.

**LabBot in practice:** Record schema_version, observation time, preprocessing version, model hash, and action specification per request. Retain only necessary permitted logs and follow the project's image-data rules.
