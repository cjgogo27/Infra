## The engineering problem

The policy predicts 16 future actions, so you invoke it only once per 16 steps. The arm becomes slow to react to changes.

> Keep this question in mind: Does predicting 16 steps require executing all 16?

**Diffusion policy · Action chunk · Receding-horizon execution**

> **In one sentence: Predicting multiple actions amortizes inference, but the replanning interval determines feedback freshness.**

A diffusion action head generates a continuous action sequence through iterative denoising. Flow-matching approaches have distinct objectives and samplers; they are not all the same diffusion algorithm. Sampling steps, sequence length, and architecture affect cost. Fewer steps may accelerate inference but require quality evaluation.

A predicted chunk need not be executed completely. Predict H steps, execute the first K, then observe and replan. Larger K reduces invocation frequency but extends open-loop operation. Smaller K gives fresher feedback but requires sufficient inference speed.

Asynchronous inference computes the next chunk while the robot executes the old one. Handle start times, already-executed prefixes, obsolete results, and smooth switching. Replacing actions whenever a computation finishes may use a chunk whose time origin has already passed.

Break down conditional-feature computation, the iterative network, sampler updates, and denormalization. Features unchanged across iterations may be reusable; verify cacheability for the actual architecture.

**Upstream and downstream:** Policy frequency is not servo frequency. A low-rate policy may provide goals while a high-rate controller tracks them.

**LabBot in practice:** Log predicted horizon, executed horizon, replanning time, action discontinuities, and success in simulation before testing caching or fewer denoising steps.

Use chunk_start_ms and now_ms in [LabBot replay](/Infra/en/labs/06-replay/) to reject outdated chunks. This validates a protocol, not real motion.
