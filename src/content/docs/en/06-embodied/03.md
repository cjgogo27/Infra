## The engineering problem

GPU updates are fast but the RL iteration is slow. Resets, rendering, and copies dominate, and some environments repeatedly fail.

> Keep this question in mind: Why can more parallel environments reduce useful training efficiency?

**Rollout · Vectorized environments · Policy lag**

> **In one sentence: Robot RL infrastructure coordinates environments, policies, and data; fresh useful samples matter more than busy GPUs.**

An iteration includes observation, inference, stepping, rewards, termination/reset, trajectory assembly, and updates. Vision may be rendering-bound; complex contacts may be simulation-bound; device copies may dominate. Measure environment steps/s and useful samples/s before training tokens/s.

Parallel environments increase throughput and also state, rendering-buffer, and trajectory memory. Unequal resets cause imbalance. Organize data by environment and time, distinguish termination from time truncation, and construct bootstrap values and masks correctly. Otherwise faster sampling can yield incorrect learning signals.

Asynchronous rollout overlaps sampling and training but may use an outdated policy. Record policy versions and lag, controlling freshness according to the algorithm. Old trajectories are not automatically valid for arbitrary on-policy updates.

Stability includes NaNs, exploding gradients, abnormal rewards, stuck environments, and leaks. Preserve configuration, seed, environment ID, sample summaries, and versions around failures. Restarting without evidence does not establish that a fault is fixed.

**Upstream and downstream:** More environments can alter sampling and optimization. Lower simulation fidelity can change physics, so speed alone is insufficient.

**LabBot in practice:** Compare state-only environments without rendering against visual environments to isolate rendering costs. Record every simplification and reevaluate policy quality.
